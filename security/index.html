How your vault is protected

Affluenter Vault for Android · Updated November 21, 2026

This page explains in plain language what protects your vault, what leaves your phone, and what happens if something goes wrong. It describes how the app is built today. The Privacy Policy is the legal document.

The short version

  • Your vault is encrypted on your phone with AES-256-GCM. Affluenter never receives it. There is no account and no Affluenter server holding your data.
  • The key comes from your master password, stretched with Argon2id. We can’t see it and we can’t reset it.
  • The app has no ads, no analytics and no third-party trackers. Optional online features send only the small amounts listed below.
  • No outside security firm has reviewed the app yet. See “What we can’t promise”.

How the vault is locked

Your vault is one encrypted file on your phone. Logins, cards, notes, 2FA secrets and passkeys are all inside it.

  • A random 256-bit key encrypts the vault with AES-256-GCM, which also notices any change to the file. The file’s header is checked as well, so it can’t be quietly altered.
  • That random key is itself locked with a second key made from your master password using Argon2id, a slow, memory-hard method that makes guessing expensive. On most phones it uses 64 MB of memory, and the number of passes is tuned so unlocking takes about a second on your phone.
  • Argon2id is built into the app and is tested against the published test vector of the standard (RFC 9106).
  • Secure Files (scans, photos, documents) are encrypted with the same key and AES-256-GCM, in 1 MB pieces.
  • Vaults made before version 1.1.31 used an older method (PBKDF2). The app asks for your master password once to upgrade them to Argon2id.
  • Your master password is never stored. A long, unique master password is what keeps someone out who has a copy of the file.

Unlocking without typing the master password

Fingerprint

Android’s own fingerprint check does the matching. The app never sees your fingerprint.

PIN

Your PIN is stretched with Argon2id and combined with a second key kept in your phone’s secure hardware (Android Keystore, in the dedicated security chip when the phone has one). That hardware key only works while the phone itself is unlocked, so a copy of the app’s files is useless on another device. After 5 wrong PINs the PIN is erased and the master password is needed. New PINs need at least 6 digits and can’t be an obvious pattern.

While the app is open

  • It locks after 60 seconds away and when the screen turns off. Both can be changed in Settings.
  • Screenshots and the recent-apps preview are blocked by default.
  • Copied passwords and codes are marked sensitive and cleared from the clipboard after 30 seconds by default. Some keyboards keep their own clipboard history; you can clear it or turn it off in the keyboard’s settings.
  • Android’s cloud backup includes only the encrypted vault file, never the app’s settings or the fingerprint key.

What leaves your phone

Only when you use a feature that needs it. The Privacy Policy has the full list. In short:

  • Breach check: the first 5 characters of a password’s SHA-1 hash go to Have I Been Pwned, never the password.
  • 2FA site list: the app downloads a public list from 2fa.directory and sends nothing about you or your vault.
  • Change-password check: asks that one website whether it has a standard change-password page.
  • Passkey checks: use Google’s public lists of trusted apps and browsers.
  • Email masking (Pro): talks to the service you connected, such as DuckDuckGo, SimpleLogin, addy.io or Firefox Relay.
  • Drive sync (Pro): saves an encrypted sync file in your own Google Drive.
  • Buying Pro and update prompts: go through Google Play.

The app asks Android for these permissions: Internet, fingerprint, notifications, start-up, network status and Google Play billing. It does not ask for camera, contacts, location or storage access. Card and ID scanning uses Google’s scanner screens, and the photo is read on your phone.

If your phone is lost or stolen

  • The vault is encrypted. Someone holding your phone needs your master password, or your fingerprint or PIN on an unlocked phone.
  • Use your phone’s find-and-erase tool to lock or wipe it.
  • On a new phone, restore an encrypted backup or Drive sync (Pro) with your master password. Keep a recent backup somewhere other than the phone.
  • If the phone may have been unlocked when it was lost, change the passwords for your most important accounts first.

If you forget your master password

  • Nobody can reset it, including us, because we never receive it.
  • If fingerprint or PIN unlock still works, open the vault that way and make a fresh emergency backup (More → Settings → Emergency Access).
  • An emergency backup has its own passphrase and can be restored when you set up the app again.
  • Plan ahead: Emergency Access makes a printable Emergency Kit where you can write the passphrase down.

What we can’t promise

  • No outside audit yet. The encryption methods are standard (AES-GCM, Argon2id), but the app itself has not been reviewed by an independent security firm. If that changes, this page will say so.
  • The code is not public, so you can’t read it yourself.
  • A weak master password is the weak point. No encryption can protect a short or reused master password from someone who has a copy of your file and unlimited time to guess.
  • A compromised phone. Malware, or someone watching your screen while the vault is open, can see what you see. Keep Android updated and install apps only from sources you trust.
  • Android only, from a small company. Affluenter Vault is made by Affluenter LLC in Chicago. It is in open testing on Google Play (US).
